# Move through time

[All pages](README.md) · Previous: [Getting started](01-getting-started.md) · Next: [Map tools](03-map-tools.md)

The model runs from 2007 to the present. This page shows how to choose the time span on the chart, jump to any day,
and step or animate the map through time.

## Hourly, daily or monthly: 1H, 1D, 1M

The three buttons beside the chart set its time resolution:

| Button | Each point is | The chart spans |
|---|---|---|
| **1H** | one hour | two weeks |
| **1D** | a daily mean | one year |
| **1M** | a monthly mean | the whole record, about twenty years |

The map follows the same resolution: in 1D it shows daily means, and in 1M monthly means. Use 1M to see long-term
patterns, 1D for seasons and events lasting days to weeks, and 1H for tides and daily cycles.

## Jump to a date

Click the **dates** above the chart to open the calendar.

- **Previous year / Next year** and **Previous month / Next month** move the calendar.
- Click a day, then **OK**.

The chart moves to a window ending on that day: a year in 1D, two weeks in 1H.

## Show a time on the map

**Click the chart** to put the map on that time. In 1D the click snaps to the nearest day, and in 1H to the nearest
hour. The **MAP** line on the chart moves to show the new time, and the vertical profile follows.

## Step and Play

- **Forward one** and **Back one** step the map by one hour, day or month, depending on the resolution.
- **Play** animates the map through time, and **Pause** stops it.
- The **speed** button beside Play sets how fast it plays (see [Map tools](03-map-tools.md)).

The map and the vertical profile both update at each step.

> **Tip:** press **Auto Color** before playing. The colour range then stays fixed, so the colours show real changes
> in the water.

The first time you view a day or month in 1D or 1M, its map takes a few seconds to prepare. After that it loads
almost at once.

## Download

**Download** saves the values on the chart as CSV at the resolution shown: hourly, daily or monthly.

## Worked example: the 2021 heat dome

In late June 2021 a heat dome brought record temperatures to the Pacific Northwest. You can see it in the water.

1. Keep **Field** on *Temperature* and zoom in on the central Strait of Georgia, around 49.2° N, 123.5° W.
2. Press **1M** to see twenty years of monthly means, then **1D**.
3. Click the dates and go to **29 June 2021**. The chart now shows Dec 29, 2020 – Dec 29, 2021.
4. Hover over late June: the surface warmed from about 14 °C in mid-June to about 21 °C on 29 June and 22 °C by
   early July, well above the blue Climatology band.
5. Click the chart on 29 June to put the map on that day, then press **Auto Color**.
6. Step forward a day at a time, or press **Play**, and watch the warm water spread across the Strait while the
   profile shows the warm layer at the surface.
