# Cross-section

[All pages](README.md) · Previous: [Time–depth view](06-time-depth.md) · Next: [Profiler casts](08-profiler-casts.md)

A **Cross-Section** slices the water along a line you draw on the map, and shows the Field from the surface to the
seabed all along it, at the map's time.

## Bathymetry Contours

**Bathymetry Contours** in the toolbar draws lines of equal seafloor depth on the map. They show channels, basins and
shallow banks, and help you place a line along or across them.

## Draw a line

1. Open the **Cross-Section** tab in the bottom panel.
2. **Click** the map to add points.
3. **Double-click** or press **Enter** to finish.

A line can have two points or many. Add more to follow a channel or go around a headland: the section follows every
bend.

## Read the section

The section has distance along the line across, and depth down. Each cell is coloured by the Field's value.

- **Numbered markers** on the section match the numbered points of the line on the map, so you can see where each
  leg starts.
- The **seafloor** along the line is the section's lower edge.
- The depth axis is stretched near the surface, where the water changes most.
- Hover to read distance, depth and value.

The section shows the map's time. Step or click the chart to change the time, and the section follows.

## Colours

The section uses the map's colours. **Color Settings** and **Auto Color** recolour both. Narrow the range to bring out
small differences in deep water, or widen it to see the whole column.

## New line

**New line** clears the line so you can draw another.

## Download

**Download** saves the section as CSV: the value at each distance and depth.

## Worked example: where the Fraser River meets the sea

River water is low in alkalinity, and seawater high, so Total Alkalinity traces how the Fraser River mixes into the
Strait.

1. Set **Field** to *Total Alkalinity* and zoom in on the Fraser delta.
2. Go to early June 2026, when the river runs high, and click the chart at **4 June, 16:30**. Press **Auto Color**:
   the dark area is low-alkalinity river water off the delta.
3. Turn on **Bathymetry Contours**.
4. Open **Cross-Section** and draw a line from the river mouth (49.10° N, 123.27° W) out into the deep Strait
   (49.35° N, 123.80° W). Press Enter.
5. Read the section:
   - Within 18 km of the mouth, river water forms a layer a few metres thick, with surface alkalinity of about 950
     to 1150 mmol/m³.
   - Farther out it is already mixing with seawater: about 1350 to 1900 at the surface.
   - Below 10 m, seawater fills the Strait: about 2030 to 2180 mmol/m³.
6. In **Color Settings**, set the range to **1900 – 2200**. The seawater below now shows its own layers: about 2050 at
   10 m, rising to about 2170 at the seabed.
7. Press **New line** and draw four points: 48.95° N 123.40° W → 49.22° N 123.40° W → 49.30° N 123.65° W → 49.45° N
   123.90° W. The section follows every bend, and the seafloor rises from 330 m to under 100 m on the third leg.
